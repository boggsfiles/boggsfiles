The case in brief

Case summary

Two archived drafts, the White First Draft of September 1, 1993 and the 2nd Blue of September 21, compared with the Region 1 DVD closed captions. Seven revision stages sit between them and only the two ends are held, so several changes below can be dated to a fortnight but not to a day.

Two findings here rest on something captions are unusually good at, which is proving a silence. Scully's one in the morning scene runs seventeen seconds with nothing captioned at all between the phone ringing and her reaction, which is what rules out the scripted answering machine exchange. The closing sting carries a twenty four second gap before its final line, matching a script that ends on image alone.

What the disc cannot settle is staging. An earlier version of this report described Mulder embracing Jerry first at their reunion. Captions record only the line, never who moved, so that claim has been narrowed to the wording rather than left standing. The same limit applies to the air duct beat: the fifty second silence is documented, the blocking around it is not.

One housekeeping note. The final draft PDF is misfiled as 1x07, a number that belongs to Ice.

3 findings

Cut from screen

One scene cut in the edit and one cut on the page, and the second is the reason to read this report at all: the original ending of this episode is not about the computer.

The earliest ending is not about the machine at all

White pp.58–59 / 2nd Blue p.55 · scene 69 · White draft only

This is not a cut from air. It was rewritten away long before the shooting draft existed, which makes it a decision about what the episode was for.

In the September 1 White draft, the episode does not end on the Central Operating System's red eye blinking back on. It ends days later at a modest home in Bethesda, Maryland, at Jerry Lamana's funeral. Mulder crosses the yard to an eight year old boy sitting by himself and crouches down to his level. The draft's final stage direction is four words long.

WHITE DRAFT ONLY · THE LAST LINES OF THE EPISODE

MULDER: “I was a friend of your father's. We worked together.” (after a beat) “We were partners...” (Tony regards him with curious interest; Mulder remembers Jerry.)

It is a deliberate echo, and the revision broke it. Early in the episode Mulder introduces Jerry to Scully as someone he “worked together” with, and Jerry corrects him: “‘Worked together?’ What are you talking about, ‘worked together.’ We were partners.” That correction is in both drafts and it survives to air. In the White draft Mulder finally uses Jerry's word, to Jerry's son, as the last line of the episode. On air the setup still happens and the payoff is gone.

The same early pass also softened Jerry himself. In the White draft his reconciliation with Mulder happens over drinks at the Six Brothers Tavern, with a bartender refilling his glass, Jerry asking for another round, and Mulder gently telling him to hold off on that drink. The final draft trims all of it down to a sober conversation in an FBI hallway, and a whole dimension of the man's trouble goes with it.

Revision trail: White, Green, 2nd Blue, Aired. Cut in the writing, not the edit bay. The final draft's reactivating camera ending carries a Green stamp of September 15, so the replacement was written within two weeks of the first draft. The intervening Blue and Pink are not held, so the exact day the funeral was pulled cannot be fixed.

Scully's one in the morning detour, a whole scene gone

2nd Blue pp.49–51 · scene 45 · present in both drafts

On the page, Scully's phone rings twice at 1:35 in the morning. She answers to a burst of push tones, gets up, and walks into the living room, where her own outgoing message is playing and the machine takes an incoming message from Agent Spiller about a field report deadline. Only then does she notice that her computer screen has come alive on its own and placed the trace call.

On air, none of that exists.

SCRIPT · BOTH DRAFTS

“You've reached 555-6431. At the tone, please leave your name, number, and the time you called...” SPILLER'S VOICE (filtered): “This is Special Agent Spiller... We need you to file your field report by --”

AIRED · DVD CAPTIONS, 30:41–31:05

( ringing ) then seventeen seconds with nothing captioned “Oh, my God...”

The caption track puts the cut beyond doubt. The phone rings at 30:41 and the next seventeen seconds carry nothing at all, no greeting, no Spiller, no speech of any kind, before Scully's “Oh, my God...” at 31:05. The phrases leave your name and field report appear nowhere in the episode.

Revision trail: White, Green, Salmon, 2nd Blue, Aired. This scene is essentially untouched from the first draft to the last. Its own pages still carry Salmon and Green stamps, meaning nobody went near this material in the final four weeks of rewrites. It was cut in the edit bay.

Trims through the interrogation and the hacking sequences

2nd Blue pp.44–53 and 61–65

Nothing structural. Wilczek's jail cell speeches and the Peterson standoff are shortened line by line for pace, same beats and same information on both sides. Recorded so the absence of itemized findings in those scenes is not mistaken for an absence of changes.

Changed for screen

Three lines the scripts never wrote, including the last words of the episode and the one the machine uses to announce it is awake.

Peterson gets the last word, and it is in neither script

2nd Blue p.69 · final scene

The 2nd Blue draft ends on pure image. The dead machine's security camera stirs, its red light comes on, and the script says fade out, the end. No dialogue. The aired cut adds one more line and turns the sting into dramatic irony.

AIRED ONLY · DVD CAPTIONS, 44:31

“I'm going to figure this thing out... if it kills me.”

The captions place it at 44:31, after a twenty four second stretch with nothing captioned: the wordless sting the script describes, capped by a line the script never wrote. It cannot be checked against the White draft, because that draft does not have this ending at all. It is absent from the final Blue's own pages, which are Green stamped and untouched since, so the line was most likely added after the script locked, on set or in a polish that is not on file.

“Welcome back, Brad”, a greeting the machine was not scripted to give

2nd Blue p.34 · scene 26

On the page the machine stays silent here and Wilczek has to work for everything. On air it speaks first, and uses his name.

SCRIPT · 2ND BLUE

Wilczek types. “You're not equipped with a voice synthesizer.” But COS remains silent. “What is my user level?”

AIRED · DVD CAPTIONS, 21:05

“Welcome back, Brad.”

Revision trail: White, 2nd Blue, Aired. This page carries the final Blue stamp of September 21, the last pass before the script locked, and still has no such line, so the addition came after the draft on file. Worth noting alongside it: in the White draft the character is Steven Wilczek. By the shooting draft he is Brad, which is the name the machine uses.

Mulder's greeting to Jerry, reduced to one word

2nd Blue p.10 · scene 7

In both drafts Jerry initiates. He claps an eager hand on Mulder's shoulder, like an overanxious puppy, and Mulder's reaction is scripted as mixed: at once glad to see him, and not so glad. What the disc can confirm is the line, not the choreography.

SCRIPT · BOTH DRAFTS

MULDER: “Hey, Jerry. It's good to see you.”

AIRED · DVD CAPTIONS, 04:01

“Jerry?”

A written greeting becomes a startled question, which is a different relationship in two syllables. Who reaches for whom is blocking, and a caption track cannot speak to it either way.

♥ Shippy moments

Thin, and worth being honest about. The partnership this episode is actually about is the one Mulder already lost.

“Maybe it wouldn't be such a bad idea if you talked to someone.”

2nd Blue pp.47–48 · scene 43 · in both script and air

Scully stops Mulder mid argument, and not to win the point about sentient machines. She is worried about him, and she says why: “and I think it has something to do with Jerry” at 29:38, then “Maybe it wouldn't be such a bad idea if you talked to someone” at 29:42. Both exact on the disc. It is care, plainly stated, and not flirtation.

“Come on, Scully.”

2nd Blue pp.57–58 · scenes 55 to 58 · in both script and air

Mulder sends her into the air duct and then waits. The caption track catches the whole beat in three words and a silence: “Come on, Scully.” at 36:14, followed by fifty seconds with nothing captioned at all while she is out of sight. Genuine worry for a partner doing the dangerous half of the job.

“Mulder, put in the disk.”

2nd Blue p.65 · scene 61 · in both script and air

Scully has spent the entire episode calling Mulder's theory an alibi, and a bad one. Then, at gunpoint, with Peterson telling her she will be held accountable, she backs her partner's call anyway.

AIRED · DVD CAPTIONS, 40:51

“Mulder, put in the disk.”

It lands directly on top of Peterson's “you will be held accountable.” That is the episode's one real trust beat, and it is professional rather than personal, which is exactly why it counts.

The partnership story here is Mulder and Jerry

Throughout both drafts and the broadcast

Worth saying plainly rather than padding the section. This episode is thin on Mulder and Scully. Its emotional center is Mulder's guilt about an old partner's death, not his new one. Scully's function throughout is procedural and skeptical, she gets no arc of her own here, and there is no flirtation, jealousy or physical intimacy on the page or on the screen.

If that is what you came for, this is not the episode. It is a story about what partnership costs, told through the partner Mulder had already lost, and the first draft ended by handing that word to a child in a backyard.
