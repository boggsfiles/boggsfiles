The case in brief

Case summary

Two archived drafts, the White First Draft of August 13, 1993 and the Gold of September 1, compared with the Region 1 DVD closed captions.

The big cut came in the writing. On the White draft, standing at Howard Graves' grave, Scully tells Mulder about calling her mother from boarding school and the day she stopped saying “I love you” before hanging up. The Gold page that replaces it carries an August 31 Green stamp, so it was gone within two weeks. The big addition came in the edit: a scene of Scully typing a field report in voiceover at her apartment, thirty seconds of it, on no page.

Stage directions are reported from the page only; the captions carry speech and bracketed sound cues, never blocking. The disc spells the antagonist DORLUND at 08:22, 35:45 and 41:14, matching both drafts, so there is no name discrepancy to report.

Every aired line below carries its timecode, so any of it can be checked against the disc.

Cut from screen

The graveside confession, gone by the Gold, and the CIA's immunity offer, gone in the edit.

Scully's cemetery confession, cut before the final draft

White pp.31–32 · scene 32 · White draft only

In the White draft, while the agents watch Lauren at Howard Graves' grave, Scully tells Mulder a story he did not ask for. Away at private school as a teenager, she and her mother ended every phone call the same way, until the day she was too embarrassed to say it and her mother stopped saying it back. Neither of them ever got the habit back. Mulder puts a hand on her shoulder, and that is the end of it.

By the Gold draft the scene has been stripped to nothing but a line at a headstone and an exchange with the Groundskeeper. The confession is gone, and so is the gesture that answered it.

WHITE DRAFT ONLY

SCULLY: “When I was a teenager, I went away to a private school. And whenever I talked to my Mom on the phone and it was time to say goodbye... one of us would say ‘I love you’ and the other would say ‘I love you, too.’ ... So one time, I didn't say it and I was too embarrassed to explain why. The next time, she didn't say it either. ... And now... I'd give anything for one perfunctory goodbye.” (Mulder puts a friendly hand on her shoulder.)

Revision trail: White, Blue, Pink, Green, Yellow, Gold, Aired. It survived barely two weeks. It is there word for word in the August 13 White, but the Gold page that replaces it carries an August 31 Green stamp, so it was already gone by that revision. Which pass cut it, Blue or Pink, is not on file. It never reached the shooting draft.

The caption track agrees: the words I love you appear nowhere in the episode. This was a decision made on paper, not in the edit.

The CIA offers Lauren immunity, and Mulder refuses it for her

Gold p.50 / White p.49 · scene 46 · present in both drafts

In every draft on file, Agents Webster and Saunders offer Lauren immunity and protection in exchange for her cooperation, and Mulder answers for her before she can. The aired cut drops the offer and the refusal together.

SCRIPT · GOLD

SAUNDERS: “In exchange for her cooperation, we're prepared to offer immunity and protection.” MULDER: “She doesn't need your protection.”

AIRED · DVD CAPTIONS, 33:50–33:56

“We don't have enough evidence to hold her. If she doesn't talk, she goes free and we lose our chance to break this company.” (no offer, no refusal)

The captions settle it twice over. The words immunity and protection appear nowhere in the episode, and the scene runs unbroken from “We don't have enough evidence to hold her” at 33:50 through “we lose our chance to break this company” at 33:56, with no gap the exchange could sit in.

Revision trail: White, Blue, Pink, Green, Yellow, Gold, Aired. Present in the very first draft and still there, trimmed but intact, in the Gold final. This one was not rewritten away. It was cut in the edit.

Trims through the FBI sweep and the interrogation

Gold pp.40–49 and 50–54

Nothing structural. The warrant briefing, the search of HTG's offices and Lauren's interrogation are all shortened line by line for pace, with the same beats and the same information on both sides. Listed so the record shows the scenes were checked.

Changed for screen

Thirty seconds of voiceover on no page, and a closing exchange with a different joke on every draft.

Thirty seconds of Scully with no script behind it

Not in White or Gold · falls around Gold p.42, between scenes 40 and 41

Between the tissue bank results and the night Lauren is attacked, the aired cut inserts a whole scene that exists on no page: Scully at her apartment, typing a field report in voiceover about Lauren's estrangement from her family. Neither draft has this scene or anything like it.

It reads like something written for the edit, to bridge two plot points the scripts connect with a straight cut.

AIRED ONLY · DVD CAPTIONS, 21:34–22:04

“Further investigation into Lauren Kyte's personal history... reveals an estrangement from her family. Phone records confirm no contact with her parents for the last two years. Her actions, observed during surveillance, indicate a strong relationship between Lauren Kyte... and her employer, the late Howard Graves. Was this relationship somehow the motivation for his suicide? How are the attack and subsequent murders of the Isfahan agents related, if at all? I am certain the answers to these questions... lie in finding the identity of Lauren Kyte's accomplice.”

The caption track carries the whole thing in the clear across a thirty second stretch beginning at 21:34. It is the single largest piece of aired dialogue in this episode with no source on the page at all.

The closing exchange, re-punchlined at every stage on record

Gold pp.65–67 · scene 55

Mulder and Scully's exchange in the driveway after Lauren drives off was rewritten between every version this report has evidence for. Not trimmed: given a different joke each time.

SCRIPT · WHITE

MULDER: “Do you believe in a life after this one, Scully?” SCULLY: “I was taught to, but it doesn't make the thought of dying any easier.”

SCRIPT · GOLD

MULDER: “You believe in an afterlife, Scully.” SCULLY: “Sure. But personally the jury's still out on revenge from the grave.”

AIRED · DVD CAPTIONS, 43:33

“Hey, Scully, do you believe in an afterlife?” / “I'd settle for a life in this one.”

The scene also picks up a closing line that exists on no page in either draft. The Gold ends on Mulder's smile. The captions have Scully ask “Why now?” at 43:58 and Mulder answer, as the last words of the episode, “I don't know. How late do you think they stay open?” at 44:01.

♥ Shippy moments

The confession again, the Liberty Bell, and Mulder thrown by how she gets through to Lauren.

“I love you, too”: the confession, again

White pp.31–32 · cut before Gold

Scully tells Mulder, unprompted, about losing the habit of saying “I love you” to her mother, and he answers by putting a hand on her shoulder and saying nothing. White draft only; see Cut from Screen.

“Why now?”

Gold scene 55 · present in every draft, reworded in each

Mulder invents a reason to keep the afternoon going. Out of nowhere he wants to see the Liberty Bell, and will not say why. It is in every version of the script and it survives to air, just with different jokes around it each time.

The captions have the whole exchange: “You ever seen the Liberty Bell?” at 43:39, Scully's “You're not missing much. It's a big bell with a big crack and you have to wait in a long line,” his “I'd really like to go,” and her “Why now?” at 43:58.

Mulder, visibly thrown by how Scully gets through to Lauren

Gold pp.55–57 · scene 48 · in both script and air

Scully talks Lauren into helping by appealing to her grief rather than the law, telling her to say it again by showing him. It works, and Mulder is startled by it. On the disc the reaction lands as two beats ten seconds apart.

AIRED · DVD CAPTIONS, 38:11 and 38:13

“What are you doing, Scully?” ... “You don't believe.”

The Gold's stage direction: Mulder is “stunned by Scully's words and actions.” The lines aired as written; the direction cannot be checked.
